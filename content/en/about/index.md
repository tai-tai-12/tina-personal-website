My path started in medical imaging and AI research. Over time, I became interested not only in whether a model could perform well, but in what it takes for technology to work in real-world environments: defining the right problem, aligning stakeholders, validating performance, navigating constraints, and turning evidence into decisions.

That interest led me from research into product management and cross-functional execution, and then to Carnegie Mellon University's M.S. in Business Analytics program to deepen my training in analytics, machine learning, optimization, data-driven decision-making, and technology strategy.

I am especially interested in product and analytics roles involving AI and healthcare, while keeping a broader focus on technology products where rigorous analysis and cross-functional judgment matter.

Outside work, I enjoy documenting everyday life, photography, and exploring new places and food. I like projects that combine structure with curiosity: understanding how something works, then making it clearer and more useful.
