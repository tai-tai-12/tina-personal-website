My path started in medical imaging and AI research. Over time, I became less interested in model performance as an end point and more interested in the decisions around it: what users actually need, which trade-offs matter, what evidence is defensible, and how a team turns that evidence into a product that works in a real workflow.

That interest led me from research into product management and cross-functional execution, and then to Carnegie Mellon University's M.S. in Business Analytics program to deepen my training in analytics, machine learning, optimization, data-driven decision-making, and technology strategy.

I am especially interested in product and analytics roles involving AI and healthcare, while keeping a broader focus on technology products where rigorous analysis and cross-functional judgment matter.
