## Two distinct workstreams

### 1. Third-party product testing & consortium operations

I served as a primary student assistant for the **Ionizing Radiation Industry–University Technical Collaboration Consortium**, supporting member-facing operations, product-testing coordination, quality documentation, and report production. The laboratory's testing work covered items such as detector performance and X-ray-tube measurements, including DQE, MTF, NPS, focal spot, HVL, equivalent filtration, and tube voltage.

My responsibilities included:

- Coordinating third-party equipment-testing workflows and client-facing documentation.
- Producing and organizing test reports used as independent technical evidence for product review and compliance preparation.
- Supporting consortium administration, member communication, and documentation workflows.
- Maintaining quality-system records and supporting audit / assessment preparation.
- Connecting testing evidence, laboratory procedures, and final reporting so outputs were traceable and repeatable.

### 2. Quantitative CT analysis — separate research support

Separately from the product-testing work, I developed **MATLAB-based quantitative CT analysis** for small-animal bone studies. I processed micro-CT images, standardized bone-mineral-density and trabecular measurements, and supported analysis work used by **two university laboratories**.

This was an analytics/research-support workflow—not part of the third-party product-testing reports above.

## Results & impact

- Conducted or supported third-party testing across **8 ionizing-radiation equipment products**.
- Produced **10+ third-party testing / certification-support reports** across client projects.
- Supported compliance evidence preparation for **5 client organizations**.
- Participated in quality-system and audit-readiness work in a laboratory operating under **ISO/IEC 17025 / TAF accreditation** for defined scopes.
- Built MATLAB tooling adopted by **2 university laboratories** for standardized bone-mineral-density analysis.

## What I learned

This experience taught me two complementary skills: how to make **technical evidence credible and auditable**, and how to turn repeated analytical work into **standardized, reusable workflows**. Those lessons later carried directly into medical-AI validation, regulatory documentation, and product operations.
