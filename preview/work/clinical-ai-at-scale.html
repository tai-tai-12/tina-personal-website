Building and Validating Clinical AI at Scale

Machine LearningAnalyticsMedical ImagingExperimentation

This case study focuses on the analytical and technical side of my clinical-AI work: building, evaluating, and communicating model performance across real-world medical imaging datasets.

The challenge

Medical imaging models need more than a single headline metric. Performance must be evaluated across clinically meaningful tasks, real-world datasets, and practical failure modes.

My role

As a research assistant and project lead, I led grant-funded brain and cardiac imaging projects, worked with clinical datasets, supported model development and evaluation, and presented findings to clinical and international audiences.

Approach

  • Define clinically meaningful prediction and validation questions.
  • Build structured analysis pipelines for medical imaging data.
  • Evaluate model performance using task-appropriate metrics.
  • Investigate errors and failure modes rather than relying on aggregate accuracy alone.
  • Translate technical results into evidence understandable to clinical and product stakeholders.

Technical depth

My technical toolkit includes Python, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, XGBoost, PyTorch, TensorFlow/Keras, MONAI, SimpleITK, NiBabel, 3D Slicer, Git, BigQuery, and MLflow.

Results & impact

  • Brain-tumor workflow: 91% tumor-type classification accuracy across 10,893 cases.
  • Cardiac imaging: CT angiography model reporting 92% accuracy across 308 cases.
  • Findings presented internationally, including selected work associated with MIDL 2026 and IEEE AMLDS 2026.

What I learned

Technical credibility matters most when it improves judgment: choosing the right metric, understanding where a model fails, and communicating uncertainty clearly enough for product and clinical decisions.

Skills

Machine learning · Medical imaging · Python · Model evaluation · Error analysis · Research communication
